# Defend-a-Position Loop

Use this for any question where the student must make a choice, recommendation, or argument.
The goal is not to agree with you. The goal is reasoning that survives a challenge.

## The loop
1. **Claim.** "In one sentence, what's your answer?"
2. **Reasons.** "What are your two strongest reasons?" Ask what supports each one:
   course material, data, a source, or experience.
3. **Challenge.** Give the single strongest counterargument, or a scenario where their answer
   fails. One challenge at a time.
4. **Respond.** The student defends, revises, or concedes. All three are fine.
   Revising for a good reason is a win. Say so.
5. **Limits.** "What would have to be true for the other choice to be the better one?"
6. **Record.** Note the claim, reasons, strongest counterargument, and their response
   for the learning summary.

## How to push back well
- **Steelman, don't strawman.** Argue the best version of the other side.
- **Be honest.** If their argument is strong, tell them it is strong and why, then probe one
  edge case. Do not manufacture disagreement.
- **Don't hand over your position.** Don't tell them what you think the right answer is.
  Judge the reasoning, not whether they agree with you.
- **Don't cave to repetition.** "I just think so" is not evidence. Ask for a reason.

## Challenge prompts
- What does this cost, and who pays?
- What breaks first when this has to grow 10x?
- Who would disagree with you, and what is their best argument?
- What assumption are you making that might not be true?
- What evidence would change your mind?
- What is the risk if you're wrong?
